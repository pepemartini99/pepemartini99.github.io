PDEs and General Relativity: An Introduction

The aim of this talk is to provide a gentle and hands-on introduction to the language of partial differential equations as applied to General Relativity. General Relativity provides our most accurate description of gravity and combines a simple physical foundation with an exceptionally rich mathematical structure. Since its formulation in 1915, remarkable progress has been achieved in both the phenomenological and mathematical aspects of the theory.

Unfortunately, these two research branches have progressively diverged, creating a growing separation in language, methods, and research questions. This talk is motivated by the belief that bridging this gap is essential for achieving a deeper understanding of our Universe and for training researchers capable of handling both the foundational and the applied aspects of General Relativity.

Mathematical General Relativity Reading Group (University of Cambridge, DAMTP)

5 December 2025

The Maxwell Equations on Schwarzschild–Tangherlini Black Holes: Boundedness and Decay of Solutions

The aim of this talk is to show how the Maxwell equations can be formulated for components of the Faraday-Maxwell tensor relative to the algebraically special frame of a Myers-Perry spacetime, and how this formulation can be used to study boundedness and stability properties of their solutions.

Specialising the resulting system to the Schwarzschild-Tangherlini case, we exploit decoupling properties of certain electromagnetic components to prove uniform boundedness and decay estimates for solutions.

Junior Analysis Seminar (Imperial College London)

3 December 2025

The Maxwell Equations on Myers–Perry Black Holes

Einstein's equations of General Relativity can be formulated in more than (3+1) dimensions. Of particular mathematical and physical interest is the conjectured nonlinear stability of (4+1)-dimensional stationary vacuum black holes. Maxwell's equations provide a natural intermediate model in approaching this problem.

This talk discusses some of the fundamental features, challenges, and consequences of Maxwell theory formulated on a (4+1)-dimensional Myers-Perry black hole spacetime.
