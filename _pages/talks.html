Christodoulou, intended for an audience already familiar with General Relativity. --- ### Theoretical Physics Journal Club (University of Cambridge, DAMTP) **10 December 2025** **PDEs and General Relativity: An Introduction** The aim of this talk is to provide a gentle and hands-on introduction to the language of PDEs applied to General Relativity. General Relativity provides to date our most accurate description of gravity. It combines a simple and concrete physical foundation with an incredibly rich mathematical structure. Since its formulation in 1915, huge progresses have been made concerning both the phenomenological and mathematical aspects of the theory. Unfortunately, these two research branches have parted ways over the years: as a result, there is a growing gap in their languages, in the problem they investigate and even in the reasons to investigate them. This talk is motivated by the belief that bridging this gap, and hence having people who can handle both the foundational and the more concrete aspects of General relativity, is essential to achieve a deeper understanding of our Universe. --- ### Mathematical General Relativity Reading Group (University of Cambridge, DAMTP) **5 December 2025** **The Maxwell Equations on Schwarzschild–Tangherlini Black Holes: Boundedness and Decay of Solutions** The aim of this talk is to show how to formulate the Maxwell equations for components of the Faraday--Maxwell tensor relative to the algebraically special frame of a Myers--Perry spacetime, and how it is possible to use this form of the equations to discuss boundedness and stability of their solutions. We then specialise the system of equations to the Schwarzschild--Tangherlini case and use special decoupling properties properties of some of the electromagnetic components to prove uniform boundedness and decay of solutions. --- ### Junior Analysis Seminar - Imperial College London **3 December 2025** **The Maxwell Equations on Myers–Perry Black Holes** Einstein’s Equations of General Relativity can be formulated in more than (3+1) dimensions. Of particular interest both from the mathematical and astrophysical point of view is the conjectured nonlinear stability of (4+1)- dimensional stationary (spherical) vacuum black holes. Maxwell’s Equations represent a natural intermediate model to be studied towards the comprehension of this problem: this talk will deal with some fundamental features, challenges and consequences of Maxwell’s theory formulated on a (4 + 1)-dimensional Myers–Perry black hole spacetime.
